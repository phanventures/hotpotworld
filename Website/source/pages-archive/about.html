There is no sincerer love than the love of food.

A dining room built around a simple idea. Give people good ingredients, a pot of boiling broth, and let them cook their own dinner.

Dinner cooked at the table, not in a kitchen.

Hot pot is an East Asian way of eating: a simmering pot of stock set right on your table, and a spread of raw ingredients you cook in it yourself, a few pieces at a time.

There are three parts to it. The broth you cook in. The ingredients you dip. The sauce you dip them into afterwards. Everything else is up to you.

It is also, quietly, one of the healthier ways to eat out. Nothing is deep fried or drowned in sauce before it reaches you. It is vegetables, mushrooms, seafood and thin cut meat, cooked in broth, seasoned by you.

We did not pick one country.

Most hot pot restaurants commit to a single tradition and cook within it. We did the opposite. Our broths come from across Asia: Vietnam, China, Thailand, Korea and beyond, sitting on the same menu.

Then there is the belt. Instead of a checklist and a pencil, we run a conveyor around the dining room stacked with fresh plates under clear lids. You see the food before you take it. Nothing waits on a kitchen.

And every seat gets its own pot and its own burner, which means a table of six can eat six different dinners without anybody compromising.

When we opened, there was nothing else like it in the Seattle area.
